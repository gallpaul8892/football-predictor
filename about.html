About this site

Football Predictor is a hobby project that started with one simple question: can a few plain rules about form, home advantage and league position predict football results better than chance? And just as important: can you see, openly, how often it gets them right?

Plenty of sites will tell you who's going to win this weekend. Very few show you their record afterwards. This one does. Every prediction is made before kick-off, saved, and then marked right or wrong once the final whistle goes. The accuracy pages show the full picture, broken down by league, by team and by week, including the weeks where it does badly.

What's covered

  • England: Premier League, Championship, League One, League Two, National League, National League North and South
  • Scotland: Premiership, Championship, League One, League Two
  • Europe: Bundesliga, Serie A, La Liga, Primeira Liga

What you'll find here

  • Upcoming predictions for the next few days, with each team's form, league position and a full breakdown of how the prediction was reached.
  • Strong favourites: the matches where one side stands out most clearly.
  • Last 7 days: each prediction next to the actual score.
  • Replay a date: go back to any day in the last two years and see what the model would have said.
  • League tables for every competition covered.

How it stays up to date

The site updates itself twice a day, early in the morning and late in the evening (UK time). The morning update adds the next few days of fixtures. The evening update collects the day's results and grades the predictions. You can see when it last updated under the title on the main page, and every update is listed under Run history.

How the predictions are made

In short: points for recent wins, for winning at home or away, for being higher in the table and for past wins against the same opponent, plus a tuned version that weights those factors by what has actually worked. The full explanation, with a worked example, is on the How the model works page.

Open and free

The site is free to use, with no sign-up. All of the code and every stored result and prediction are public in the project's GitHub repository. If you spot a mistake or have an idea for improving the model, you can raise it there.
